# Research: Engineered Fill and Structural Fill, US material unit prices

Researched 2026-11-05.

**Method.** Sources in order of preference: (1) public bids and contracts (city, town and county
supply bids, bid tabulations, awarded prices); (2) distributor and quarry published price lists;
(3) retail and online retail, recorded at price x 0.50 and graded D. No cost-data publishers, no
Ferguson. Every figure below was read on the opened page or PDF itself, not from a search snippet.
Engineer's estimates and installed (furnish-and-place) items are listed for context and kept out of
the medians.

**Grades.** A = several public bids/contracts that agree; B = one public bid/contract or a
distributor list; C = single distributor quote, public data over 3 years old, or non-transaction
public data; D = retail x 0.50.

**Name mapping used.** Structural Fill: structural fill, structural backfill (soil/granular), gravel
borrow, granular borrow, B borrow. Engineered Fill: engineered fill, select fill, select borrow,
select granular fill.

**Density.** No CY-to-ton conversion was needed for the recommended figures. Where one was made
(O'Leary select fill, sold per CY), the seller's own 1.20 tons/CY was used.

**Recommended figure: $13.13 per ton, grade A**: the median of the 12 material-only (at-pit/FOB)
public figures. These are the 11 pickup prices from the two Worcester gravel borrow Type b bids
(2024-12 and 2026-04), plus Brox's $7.50 FOB in the Allenstown quotes. The awarded low prices
were $10.00 (2025) and $11.00 (2026). The Irving Materials list median ($18.05, Indiana, grade B)
is higher, because it is a gross posted price before contract discounts. For delivered material,
the context median is $23.00 to $24.25 per ton (Worcester item 2), and Allenstown accepted
$11.25 delivered for a short local haul.

distributor list was found under the engineered fill names.

## Can Structural Fill follow Engineered Fill's price?

The reverse is the safer link. In practice the trade uses the two names for the same product family:
the Gravelshop page itself says select fill is "also known as Engineered Fill, Structural Fill".
The evidence is far stronger on the structural fill side: grade A, two public bid events with
9 distinct bidders, plus a 2026 town quote. Engineered Fill's own figure is grade D and is skewed low
by Texas sandy-clay select fill. Recommendation: **set Engineered Fill to follow Structural Fill at
$13.13/ton (grade A, material only)**, rather than letting Structural Fill follow the grade-D $5.00
figure. If the database must keep the two separate, store Engineered Fill at $5.00/ton grade D
and flag it as low-confidence.
